What These Trackers Track (Simple Guide)

1. Advertising Networks
Analogy: Digital Billboard

Networks like Google DoubleClick, Amazon Ads, and AppNexus embedded across thousands of websites.

What they track:
  • Articles and news you read daily
  • Your search habits, interests, and shopping categories
  • Your approximate city or region
Why they do it: Builds a commercial profile about your lifestyle so advertisers can bid on ads matching your habits.
2. Analytics & Audience Measurement
Analogy: In-Store Foot Traffic Camera

Tools like Google Analytics (GA4), Hotjar, and Microsoft Clarity hired by site owners to understand visitors.

What they track:
  • Where your mouse moves, clicks, and how far down you scroll
  • How many seconds or minutes you stay on a page
  • Your device model, browser, and operating system
Why they do it: Helps website owners see what content is popular and fix confusing pages where visitors drop off.
3. Social Media Tracking Pixels
Analogy: Personal ID Scanner

Tags placed by Meta (Facebook/Instagram), TikTok, LinkedIn, and Twitter/X on regular shopping sites and blogs.

What they track:
  • That you visited this specific site outside of social media
  • Actions you took (viewing flights, reading articles, shopping)
Why they do it: Links your off-site browsing directly to your logged-in social media profile, causing ads for items you just browsed to appear on your feed.
4. Retargeting & Conversion Trackers
Analogy: Persistent Salesperson

Specialized ad platforms like Criteo, AdRoll, The Trade Desk, and Bing Ads designed to follow you around.

What they track:
  • Exact product names, prices, and colors you looked at
  • Items you added to your cart and abandoned
Why they do it: Follows you to other websites with ads showing the exact same items you viewed, urging you to finish the purchase.
5. Device Fingerprinting Scripts
Analogy: Digital Fingerprint Scanner

The stealthiest tracking technique (e.g. FingerprintJS). Does NOT rely on cookies, so Incognito mode does not stop it.

What they track:
  • Your graphics card (GPU) model and canvas rendering quirks
  • Screen resolution, audio hardware frequencies, and installed fonts
Why they do it: Calculates a unique mathematical hash of your hardware so they recognize your device even after you clear your cookies.
6. Tracking Beacons & 1x1 Pixels
Analogy: Invisible Delivery Receipt

Microscopic 1x1 transparent image files embedded in pages and emails that are invisible to the eye.

What they track:
  • The exact millisecond you opened the page or email
  • Your internet IP address and the previous page you came from
Why they do it: Sends an instant secret ping back to their server confirming you saw the page without needing interactive buttons.
7. Unclassified Third-Party Requests
Analogy: Unverified External Helper

Connections made to external companies that are not recognized in standard public ad directories.

What they track:
  • May transmit background session tokens or telemetry
  • Loads external cross-origin executable scripts
Why we show it: Often innocent helper tools (live chat, fonts), but audited for full transparency without labeling them malicious.
